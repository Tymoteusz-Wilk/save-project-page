SAVE: Less is More in
Agentic Video Reasoning

ICLR 2027 submission · Under double-blind review

A controlled study of video reasoning agents, a deliberately simple exploration method, and an analysis of when selective viewing helps.

Paper ↗Code ↗Walkthroughs ↓

THE PAPER / OVERVIEW

How much agent does video reasoning need?

The paper studies agentic video reasoning as a controlled comparison, then tests which design choices matter and where selective exploration is useful.

Different video agents often use different backbones and budgets, making their reported gains hard to interpret. The paper describes them through a common observe–update–act loop, then compares reimplemented agents, native video inference, and simpler alternatives under shared backbones with explicit resource accounting.
CONTROLLED COMPARISON

Complexity is no guarantee.

With strong backbones held fixed, elaborate retrieval and memory pipelines do not consistently beat native video inference. A minimal agent that asks for individual frames remains competitive with more elaborate systems.

THE METHOD

Give the agent temporal context.

The minimal baseline exposes the central bottleneck: isolated frames often lack context. SAVE instead requests a time range, samples scene-aware frames within it, adapts observation quality, and refines its search when needed.

ABLATIONS

What actually helps?

Range queries provide the largest initial improvement. Higher-quality views help tasks requiring fine visual detail, while adaptive scene sampling helps when ordinary scene detection misses useful temporal structure. Some benchmarks need mainly broad context.

WHEN TO EXPLORE

Spend attention where evidence is sparse.

Token-matched comparisons show the benefit of selective inspection grows with video length. A synthetic periodic-video study isolates temporal concentration: agents help most when the required pattern is brief relative to the whole video, and repeated refinement matters more than a single localized look.

Scope. The conclusions are based on the evaluated methods, backbones, and benchmarks. Resource comparisons track model tokens and inference cost; end-to-end latency is outside this study.
