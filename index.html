SAVE: Less is More in
Agentic Video Reasoning

Tymoteusz Wilk1Sergey Prokudin2Xi Wang2

1 Technical University of Munich2 ETH Zurich

A controlled study of video reasoning agents, a deliberately simple exploration method, and an analysis of when selective viewing helps.

Paper ↗Code ↗Walkthroughs ↓

OVERVIEW

Our contributions

Three questions motivate our work.

01 / CONTROLLED COMPARISON THE PROBLEM

Are reported agent gains really due to the agent?

Previous evaluations vary the backbone and computation budget alongside the method, making gains hard to attribute.

OUR CONTRIBUTION

We reimplement prior agents under shared backbones and datasets, measuring tokens and cost. Most do not beat native inference despite higher spend.

02 / ACTIVE EXPLORATION + SAVE THE PROBLEM

What does a good video agent actually need?

It is unclear whether elaborate agent frameworks are necessary. Could a simple exploration strategy work too?

OUR CONTRIBUTION

We test a minimal frame-querying baseline, then add range queries and scene-aware sampling to create SAVE. The baseline is competitive; SAVE leads prior agents in five of six settings at lower cost.

03 / WHEN EXPLORATION HELPS THE PROBLEM

When is agentic exploration preferable?

Our evaluation shows that even SAVE, our strongest agentic method, does not beat native inference in every setting. We ask when each approach is preferable.

OUR CONTRIBUTION

Token-matched comparisons and a controlled synthetic study show the largest gains when answer-relevant events are sparse in time.

Results

Our results across shared backbones and benchmarks. Higher performance is better; our cost comparisons measure model-side inference.
